Catakig X Support

A retro 8-bit computer emulator for the Mac.

Catakig X emulates the Apple II family of personal computers (the original Apple ][, the ][+, the //e and the //c) on Macs running macOS 14 or later, natively on Apple silicon and Intel. It is a modern revival of Catakig, rebuilt for today's Macs with Metal video and display filters, stereo sound with Mockingboard C music and speech, a mouse card and a ThunderClock-compatible clock card, game controller support, printing to PDF, saved machine states, the sounds of a real Disk II, and disks (including WOZ, ZIP and GZ files) written back in their original format. And thanks to Catakig firmware, its own original replacement for Apple's ROM, the ][+ and //e run right out of the box, with a built-in demonstration disk to try them on.

Troubleshooting & FAQ

Some problems turn up more often than others. Here are the usual suspects, and what to do about them. The same answers are in the app under Help › Troubleshooting & FAQ, and the User Guide has the whole story.

Getting Started

Do I need ROM files?

Not for the ][+ and //e. An Apple II won’t do anything at all without its ROM, and Catakig X doesn’t include any Apple ROM, because it isn’t Catakig’s to give away. Instead, it comes with Catakig firmware, its own replacement for Apple’s ROM, written from scratch, so a ][+ or //e is ready to run as soon as Catakig is installed.

The original ][ and the //c need Apple’s ROM, and so does running a ][+ or //e on Apple’s own ROM. Choose Import ROMs… from the Catakig X menu, and pick the ROM files you’ve made from an Apple II you own, or a folder holding them. Catakig copies the ones it recognizes into its own ROMs folder and tells you which models are now ready to run.

What’s the difference between Apple’s ROM and Catakig firmware?

Apple’s ROM is the real thing, so it runs everything the real machine ran. Catakig firmware is new code that does the same jobs: it starts the machine, boots disks, runs the Monitor, and has its own BASIC that works like Applesoft. Most software can’t tell the difference, but a program that jumps into the middle of Apple’s ROM, rather than using its documented entry points, may misbehave. There is firmware for the ][+ and the enhanced //e, and for the Disk II, mouse, and clock cards, but not for the original ][ or the //c, which need Apple’s ROM.

How do I choose which ROM an Apple II runs?

With the ROM pop-up in the New Apple II dialog. Once you’ve imported Apple’s ROM it’s chosen at first; pick Catakig firmware there to use that instead, and the dialog remembers your choice for next time. Until you import Apple’s ROM, the pop-up is dimmed and the ][+ and //e run on Catakig firmware. Once the Apple II is made, hold the pointer over the model emblem at the lower right of its window to see which one it runs. The choice is saved with the Apple II’s state.

A program works with Apple’s ROM but not with Catakig firmware.

Some programs, copy-protected games in particular, depend on details of Apple’s ROM that Catakig firmware doesn’t copy. If you have Apple’s ROM for that model, make a new Apple II with Apple’s ROM chosen in the ROM pop-up, and run the program there. (Changing the choice for an Apple II that already exists isn’t possible.)

Some models in the New Apple II dialog are red.

Red means there’s no ROM yet for that model. The ][ and //c run only on Apple’s ROM, so they stay red until you import it. Use the Import ROMs… button right there in the dialog, then pick the model again. Each model has ROM of its own; having one doesn’t give you the others.

I imported a file, but Catakig found no ROM in it.

Catakig recognizes the ROMs of the ][ and ][+ (12 KB), the enhanced //e (16 KB), the //c models (16 or 32 KB), and the Disk II controller card (256 bytes), as well as Catakig firmware files (their names start with “catakig-”; an imported one takes the place of the built-in one). A segment can be anywhere in its file, but it has to start at a multiple of 256 bytes. Files from other Apple II models, from a different //e revision, or with a header stuck on the front won’t be recognized. When you import a folder, only the files directly inside it are looked at, not the ones in folders within it.

Where are my ROM files kept?

In a ROMs folder inside Catakig’s own sandbox folder. Show ROM Folder in the Catakig X menu opens it in the Finder, if you want to see what’s there or remove something.

Is there anything to try it out on?

Yes: Catakig X comes with a demonstration disk. The first Apple II you make starts with it, and Open Demo Disk in the Help menu puts it back in Drive 1 of the front Apple II and restarts it (asking first if Drive 1 holds another disk). Its menu shows off graphics, sound, the Mockingboard, mouse, clock, and printer, and has a few utilities and BASIC programs; press an item’s key to choose it. It’s always write-protected, since it lives inside the application.

The Apple II window is full of static.

That’s a new Apple II waiting for its power to be turned on. Press the space bar, or any regular key, and it will start up. The power button at the lower left of the window does it too.

The screen is black, and typing does nothing.

The power is switched off. Click the power button at the lower left of the window (it’s green while the power is on), or choose Power On from the Control menu. Switching on again is a cold start, as on a real Apple II: memory is cleared and the disk in Drive 1 boots. Reset and Reboot are dimmed while the power is off.

Disks

“Failed to load disk!”

Catakig didn’t recognize the file as a 5¼-inch disk image. It loads DSK, DO, PO, NIB (and NYB), 2IMG (2MG), and WOZ files that hold 5¼-inch disks, and any of those inside a ZIP or GZ file. Some things that look like disk images but aren’t loadable yet:

• ShrinkIt and other Apple II archives, such as SHK, BXY, or SDK files. Expand them first, with a utility such as NuLib.
• ZIP files that are encrypted, or bigger than 16 MB.
• 3½-inch disks (800 KB), including Disk Copy 4.2 images, and hard-disk images. Catakig recognizes these, but has no drive to put them in.

My ZIP file holds several disks. Which one loads?

Catakig asks. When a ZIP file holds more than one disk image, a list of them comes down over the window, in name order, so you can pick the one you want. To use another disk from the same ZIP file later, load the ZIP file again and pick that one.

The disk loads, but the Apple II won’t boot it.

Disks boot only from Drive 1. Load the disk there, then click the Reboot button at the lower left of the window, or choose Reboot from the Control menu. Not every disk is bootable, either: plenty of them are data disks, which need another disk to start up first. If a DSK file boots on another emulator but not here, try renaming it to end in .po instead; a DSK file can hold either sector ordering, and Catakig has to guess which.

The disk’s name is yellow, and I can’t save anything to it.

Yellow means write-protected, and green means writeable. Catakig decides when the disk is loaded, from the file itself: a locked file (see the Finder’s Get Info window), a file you don’t have permission to change, a file on a read-only volume such as a mounted disk image, and a 2IMG file locked in its header all load write-protected. Fix the file, then load the disk again; changing it while it’s in the drive has no effect. If the same disk is loaded in two drives, only one of them can write to it.

Disks from ZIP and GZ files, and WOZ disks, always load write-protected, because Catakig can’t write changes back into them. To write to one, expand the ZIP or GZ file in the Finder and load the disk image inside it, or, for a WOZ disk, make a copy in another format with a disk utility.

My changes to a disk weren’t saved.

Catakig writes a disk’s changes back into its file when the disk is unloaded: when another disk replaces it, when you choose an Unload Drive command, or when the Apple II is closed. Not before. The disks in the frontmost Apple II stay in its drives when you quit, as part of the state Catakig saves automatically, and are written back when they’re eventually unloaded.

Catakig asks to “Save Copy as .nib” instead of saving the disk.

The software wrote tracks that a DO, PO, or 2IMG file has no way of storing. Copy-protected disks do this all the time, and so do some programs that format disks their own way. Catakig won’t mangle the original file to make the changes fit, so it offers a choice: save the whole disk, changes included, as a new NIB file (which can hold anything a real disk can), or discard the changes, or cancel and leave the disk in the drive. If you’ll be writing to a copy-protected disk often, keep a NIB copy of it and use that.

How do I insert or eject a disk without the menus?

Click a drive’s name box, below the screen, to choose a disk for that drive, or drop a disk image on it from the Finder. The eject button beside the name box takes the disk out (writing any changes back first, as the Unload Drive commands in the Peripherals menu do).

Can I see what’s on a disk image without loading it?

Yes, in the Finder. Select a disk image and press the space bar: Quick Look shows the disk’s catalog, for DOS 3.3, ProDOS, and Apple Pascal disks. Disk icons in the Finder show the catalog too, on a little green screen.

Where did my recovered disks go?

When Catakig can’t ask about a disk like that (when you quit with several Apple IIs open, for instance) it saves a NIB copy for you in its Recovered Disks folder. Show Recovered Disks in the Help menu opens it.

“Some disks couldn’t be reopened.”

A saved Apple II state remembers its disks by where they were, not by copying them. If a disk image has been moved, renamed, or deleted since the state was saved, or is on a drive that isn’t connected now, Catakig can’t get it back, and leaves that drive empty. Load the disk again yourself. (Once you do, the next saved state will remember the new location.)

Keyboard & Pasting

The F-key shortcuts don’t work, or change my screen brightness instead.

Many of Catakig’s commands are on function keys, as the Keyboard Shortcuts list shows. On most Mac keyboards, the top row controls brightness, volume, and so on unless you hold down the fn (or Globe) key. Hold it while pressing the F-key, or turn on “Use F1, F2, etc. keys as standard function keys” in System Settings › Keyboard › Keyboard Shortcuts › Function Keys. If a key is also used by a macOS shortcut (Mission Control likes F11 and F12), turn that shortcut off in System Settings, or use the menu command instead.

Where are Reset and the Apple keys?

Reset is Control-F12, which is about as close to the real Control-Reset as a Mac keyboard allows, and Reboot is Control-Command-F12. On the Mac, Command stands in for the Open-Apple key and Option for the Solid-Apple key. (So, to reboot a //e the old-fashioned way, hold Command and Control and press F12, and to run its self-test, hold Option and Control and press F12.) While Option is held, keys type their plain letters, so Option-A is Solid-Apple-A, not “å”.

A Command-key combination ran a Catakig menu command instead.

Catakig’s own shortcuts come first. Command-C, for instance, is Copy Screen Text, and Command-V is Paste Text. Programs that read Open-Apple with another key will see the ones that Catakig isn’t using.

The Delete key doesn’t erase anything.

On an Apple II, the left arrow key is the one that backs up over what you typed. The Mac’s Delete key sends the Apple’s DELETE character, which few programs pay attention to, and which the ][ and ][+ didn’t have.

Lowercase letters come out as capitals.

That’s the Apple ][ and ][+ for you: they have no lowercase, so Catakig types capitals instead. The //e and //c have lowercase, though plenty of software expects you to type in capitals anyway. Check your Caps Lock key.

Pasted text is slow, or stops partway.

Paste Text types the text into the Apple II one keystroke at a time, at whatever pace the running program reads them, so a long BASIC listing can take a while. Any key you press stops the paste, and so does Reset. Choosing a faster speed in the Control menu makes it go faster. Characters an Apple II can’t type are left out, and curly quotes and dashes become plain ones.

Joysticks & Game Controllers

The joystick won’t stay put, or won’t move at all.

Choose how the joystick is controlled in Catakig’s Preferences window. With Mouse, the pointer’s position on your screen is the stick’s position. With Numeric keypad, the keypad’s 1–9 keys push the stick in their directions (5 is center), keypad 0 and . are the two buttons, and the stick springs back to center when you let go. If your Mac has no numeric keypad, choose Letter keys (the standard choice), which uses Q W E / A S D / Z X C the same way while Caps Lock is off. Joystick keys move the stick only and don’t type into the Apple II. With Game controller, the controller’s left stick drives it.

Some letters won’t type (Q, W, E, A, S, D, Z, X, C).

With Letter keys chosen in Preferences, those keys drive the joystick while Caps Lock is off. Turn Caps Lock on to type them, or choose a different joystick setting.

My game controller does nothing.

Make sure it’s connected to the Mac (by Bluetooth or USB) and shows up in System Settings › Game Controllers, and that Game controller is chosen in Catakig’s Preferences. The A (Cross) button is Open-Apple, or paddle button 0, and B (Circle) is Solid-Apple, or paddle button 1. The Menu (Options) button types Escape, which pauses many games.

The Mouse Card

The “Mouse card in slot 7” checkbox is dimmed.

The //c has no slots, so it can’t have a mouse card; the box is available for the other models. Like the Mockingboard, the card is installed when an Apple II is created, so check the box then. (Catakig X’s mouse card is its own design, with its own firmware built in, not a copy of Apple’s, so software that programs Apple’s mouse card hardware directly won’t find it.)

My Mac’s pointer disappeared.

A program has turned on the Apple II’s mouse, and draws its own pointer instead. While it’s on, moving your mouse moves the Apple II’s pointer, and clicking is the mouse button. To get your Mac’s pointer back, push the Apple II’s pointer past an edge of its screen (in full screen, the top edge), or switch to another application. If the program stops its pointer short of the edge, keep pushing the same way and your pointer comes free. It’s also free whenever a menu is open, and until you click on the Apple II’s screen again. It comes back for good when the program turns the mouse off.

The Clock Card

ProDOS doesn’t know the date.

The Apple II needs the clock card for that, and it’s installed when an Apple II is created: check Clock card in slot 5 in the New Apple II dialog. The card works like a ThunderClock Plus, which ProDOS and most clock-aware programs find by themselves, and it keeps your Mac’s local date and time. The //c has no slots, so it can’t have one.

The “Clock card in slot 5” checkbox is dimmed.

Either the model is a //c, or the clock card’s firmware (catakig-clock.rom) isn’t installed. It normally comes built in; if it’s missing, import it with Import ROMs….

How do I read the time from BASIC?

The ThunderClock way: PR#5 and IN#5, then INPUT a string. Send % first for a 12-hour time (“TUE MAY 12 4:32:55 PM”), & for a 24-hour time, or # for just the numbers. Then PR#0 and IN#0 to get the screen and keyboard back. A program that sets the clock changes only the Apple II’s time, never the Mac’s.

The joystick stopped following the mouse.

With a mouse card installed, the pointer drives the mouse card instead of the joystick. Make a new Apple II without the mouse card for games that use a joystick, or choose another joystick setting in Preferences.

Sound & Speed

There’s no sound.

Check the Mac’s volume, and which device sound is going to (the Sound section of System Settings, or Control Center). Only the frontmost Apple II runs, and only while Catakig is the frontmost application; a halted Apple II, or one in a window behind another, is silent. If you change sound devices while Catakig is running and the sound doesn’t follow, quit Catakig and open it again.

How do I turn off the disk drive noises?

Catakig plays the sounds of a real Disk II: the motor, the head stepping and rattling, and the door as disks go in and out, plus the power switch. Turn them off in the Sounds section of Catakig’s Preferences window, with the Disk drives and Power switch checkboxes.

The Apple II stops when I switch to another application.

That’s on purpose. Emulation happens only in the frontmost Apple II, and only while Catakig is in front, so that it doesn’t compete with whatever you’re doing instead. It carries on from the same spot when you come back.

The Mockingboard is silent.

The Mockingboard is installed when an Apple II is created, so the Mockingboard in slot 2 checkbox in the New Apple II dialog needs to be checked then. The //c has no slots, so it can’t have one. Most Mockingboard software searches the slots and will find it in slot 2, but a few programs look only in slot 4, where Catakig has a memory card, and those won’t find it. The speech chip is an approximation: robotic, as it was on the real card, but not exactly the same robot.

Everything runs too fast (or too slow).

The speed is in the Control menu: Normal Speed is the Apple II’s own 1 MHz. The light at the lower right of the window shows the current speed, and clicking it steps to the next one. A pause sign means the Apple II is halted, so choose Normal Speed (F6), or click the light, to get it going again.

Display

I chose Scanlines, but I don’t see any.

Scanlines need room. They appear only when the Apple II’s screen is at least three times its original height; in a smaller window you get Sharp Pixels instead. Make the window bigger, or go full screen.

How do I get out of full screen?

Choose Exit Full Screen from the View menu, or press Shift-Command-F (or Control-F9). If the Mac’s pointer is hidden by a program using the mouse card, move it to the top of the screen first, to reach the menu bar.

The screen is green.

That’s the monochrome monitor. Monochrome/Color in the Window menu (F9) switches it, and the monochrome hue is in the Preferences window, in case you’d rather have amber.

Animation isn’t smooth.

Composite Color (NTSC), in View › Display, does the most work of the display filters; try turning it off. Quitting busy applications in the background helps too.

Saving, Printing & Screen Images

“This Apple II state was saved with a different ROM.”

The state was saved while a different ROM was installed than the one there now. Catakig restores it anyway, with today’s ROM. Most software won’t notice, but programs that depend on the exact ROM version might misbehave. A state also remembers whether it ran Apple’s ROM or Catakig firmware; if that one is no longer installed, it runs on the other. If a state needs ROM for a model you haven’t imported at all, it can’t be opened until you import it.

How do I save what the Apple II printed?

Click the printer light below the screen, or choose Save Print Session… from the Peripherals menu, and pick a format. PDF (text only), the standard choice, lays the text out on Letter-size pages and opens in Preview. Plain ASCII makes a text file, Epson to PostScript keeps an Epson printer’s graphics and type styles, and Raw bytes saves exactly what the printer received. The session isn’t erased by saving; Clear Print Session in the Peripherals menu starts it afresh.

Preview won’t open my printouts.

Epson printouts are PostScript files, and Preview stopped opening PostScript in macOS 13. For text, save the session as PDF (text only) instead. To keep an Epson printout’s graphics, convert the PostScript file to PDF with a tool such as ps2pdf from the free Ghostscript package.

“Nothing has been printed yet.”

The print session is empty. Most Apple II software prints to slot 1, which is where Catakig’s printer is; in BASIC, PR#1 sends output to it, and PR#0 sends it back to the screen.

Reporting a Problem

How do I report a bug?

While the problem is on the screen, choose Help > Save Bug Report. The Apple II pauses, and Catakig asks where to save a .zip file. Say in a sentence or two what happened and what you expected, then click Save. Send the file to the developer along with your report; nothing is sent automatically.

The report holds the Apple II’s complete state (so the developer can open it and see exactly what you saw), a picture of its screen, a summary of the machine, the instructions it ran most recently, the errors and warnings Catakig has shown lately, and the versions of Catakig and macOS. Code in Apple’s ROM is left out of the text. Of your files, only the disk image names appear (and the disks themselves, if you include them), plus any file named in an error message, with your home folder shortened to ~.

Leave “Include the disks in the drives” checked unless the disks are private: without them, most problems can’t be reproduced.

Starting Over

How do I reset Catakig completely?

Everything Catakig keeps for itself (its preferences, imported ROM files, automatically saved state, recovered disks, and error log) lives in one folder: Library/Containers/com.rodneythacker.CatakigX in your home folder. Quit Catakig and delete that folder to start again from scratch. Your imported ROM files go with it, so you’ll need to import them again. Your own disk images and saved states, wherever you keep them, are not touched.

Still stuck?

Email catakigx@icloud.com and describe what happened, what you expected, your Mac model and macOS version, and the version of Catakig X (from About Catakig X in the Catakig X menu). If it's about a particular disk image or program, say which one. Best of all, attach a bug report: choose Save Bug Report… from the Help menu while the problem is on the screen. Please don't send ROM files.
